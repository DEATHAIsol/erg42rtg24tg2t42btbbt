ProBioMarkets supports two main order types: Market orders and Limit orders. Understanding when to use each is crucial for effective trading.

## Market Orders

Market orders execute immediately at the best available price in the order book.

### Characteristics

- ✅ **Instant execution** - Fills immediately
- ⚡ **Fast** - No waiting for price
- ⚠️ **Price uncertainty** - May experience slippage
- 💰 **Best available price** - Takes current market price

### When to Use

Market orders are best for:

- **Quick entry/exit** when speed matters
- **Liquid markets** with tight spreads
- **Small positions** where slippage is minimal
- **Time-sensitive** trades

### Example

```
Current Yes Price: 50¢
You place: Market Buy for $10
Result: Executes immediately at ~50¢
Shares received: ~20 shares
```

## Limit Orders

Limit orders only execute at your specified price or better.

### Characteristics

- ✅ **Price control** - You set exact price
- ⏳ **May not fill** - Waits for matching price
- 🎯 **Precise entry** - Enter at desired price
- 💡 **Better prices** - Can get better than market

### When to Use

Limit orders are best for:

- **Specific entry points** you've identified
- **Price-sensitive** trades
- **Illiquid markets** where you want price control
- **Patient trading** strategies

### Example

```
Current Yes Price: 50¢
You place: Limit Buy at 45¢ for $10
Result: Waits until price drops to 45¢
If price reaches 45¢: Executes, you get ~22 shares
If price never reaches: Order doesn't fill
```

## Order Execution

### Market Order Execution

1. Order submitted
2. System finds best available price
3. Matches with order book
4. Executes immediately
5. Position created

### Limit Order Execution

1. Order submitted
2. Added to order book
3. Waits for matching price
4. Executes when price reached
5. Position created

<Info>
  Limit orders remain active until they fill, you cancel them, or the market closes.
</Info>

## Slippage

Slippage is the difference between expected and actual execution price.

### Market Order Slippage

- **Common in**: Illiquid markets, large orders
- **Minimize by**: Trading in liquid markets, smaller sizes
- **Acceptable range**: Usually 1-3% in active markets

### Limit Order Slippage

- **No slippage**: You get your price or better
- **Trade-off**: Order may not fill if price doesn't reach limit

## Best Practices

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="When to Use Market Orders">
    Use market orders when:
    - Speed is more important than exact price
    - Trading in highly liquid markets
    - Entering/exiting quickly
    - Position size is small relative to liquidity
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="When to Use Limit Orders">
    Use limit orders when:
    - You have a specific entry/exit price
    - Trading in less liquid markets
    - Price is more important than speed
    - You're patient and can wait
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Order Size Considerations">
    - **Small orders**: Market orders usually fine
    - **Large orders**: Consider limit orders to avoid moving price
    - **Very large**: May need to split into multiple orders
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Advanced Strategies

### Limit Order Laddering

Place multiple limit orders at different prices:

```
Limit Buy at 45¢: $5
Limit Buy at 40¢: $5
Limit Buy at 35¢: $5
```

This allows you to:
- Average your entry price
- Capture price movements
- Reduce impact on market

### Stop-Loss Orders

Use limit orders as stop-losses:

```
Current position: Long Yes at 50¢
Stop-loss: Limit Sell at 45¢
```

If price drops to 45¢, your position closes automatically.
