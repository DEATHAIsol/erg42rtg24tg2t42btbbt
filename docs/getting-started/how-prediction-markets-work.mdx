Understanding how prediction markets function is key to successful trading. This guide covers the mechanics, pricing, and market dynamics.

## Market Structure

### Binary Markets

Binary markets have exactly two outcomes:

```
Market: "Will Bitcoin reach $100k by 2025?"

Outcome 1: Yes (currently 45¢)
Outcome 2: No (currently 55¢)
```

The prices always sum to approximately $1.00 (100¢), representing the probability distribution.

### Market Creation

Anyone can create a prediction market by:

1. **Defining the question** clearly and unambiguously
2. **Setting resolution criteria** - how the outcome will be determined
3. **Choosing an end date** - when the market will resolve
4. **Providing initial liquidity** (optional but recommended)

<Warning>
  Markets must have clear, objective resolution criteria to avoid disputes.
</Warning>

## Pricing Mechanism

### Probability Pricing

Share prices represent the market's collective probability assessment:

- **75¢ Yes** = 75% chance the event occurs
- **25¢ No** = 25% chance the event occurs

### Order Book

Markets use an **order book** system:

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Bids (Buy Orders)">
    Traders who want to **buy** shares place bids. The highest bid price is the best price to sell at.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Asks (Sell Orders)">
    Traders who want to **sell** shares place asks. The lowest ask price is the best price to buy at.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Spread">
    The difference between the best bid and best ask is the "spread". Tighter spreads indicate more liquid markets.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

### Example Order Book

```
Yes Shares:
Bids (Buy):     Asks (Sell):
$0.45 (100)     $0.48 (50)
$0.44 (200)     $0.49 (100)
$0.43 (150)     $0.50 (75)

Current Price: $0.465 (midpoint)
Spread: $0.03 (3¢)
```

## Trading Mechanics

### Buying Shares

When you buy "Yes" shares:
1. You pay the current market price
2. You receive shares that pay $1.00 if the event happens
3. You can sell these shares anytime before resolution

### Selling Shares

When you sell "Yes" shares:
1. You receive the current market price
2. You're effectively betting against the event
3. You profit if the event doesn't happen

### Market Orders vs Limit Orders

## Market Dynamics

### Information Flow

Markets react to new information:

1. **News breaks** about the event
2. **Traders update** their probability assessments
3. **Prices adjust** to reflect new information
4. **Arbitrage opportunities** may arise

### Liquidity

Liquidity refers to how easily you can buy or sell without moving the price:

- **High liquidity**: Large order books, tight spreads, easy to trade
- **Low liquidity**: Small order books, wide spreads, harder to trade

<Info>
  Markets with more liquidity typically have more accurate prices because more information is incorporated.
</Info>

### Market Efficiency

Efficient markets quickly incorporate all available information into prices. Factors affecting efficiency:

- **Number of traders** - More traders = more information
- **Liquidity** - Easier trading = faster price discovery
- **Information availability** - Public information is quickly reflected

ProBioMarkets supports **leverage trading**:

<Warning>

### Leverage Example

Without leverage:
- Buy 10 shares at 50¢ = $5.00
- If price goes to 60¢: Profit = $1.00 (20% return)

With 5x leverage:
- Buy 50 shares at 50¢ = $5.00 (same capital)
- If price goes to 60¢: Profit = $5.00 (100% return)
- If price goes to 40¢: Loss = $5.00 (100% loss)

## Parlays

Combine multiple markets into a single bet:

```
Parlay:
- Market A: Yes (60¢)
- Market B: Yes (70¢)
- Market C: No (55¢)

Combined Odds: 0.60 × 0.70 × 0.55 = 0.231 (23.1¢)
```

All outcomes must be correct for the parlay to win, but payouts are multiplied.
